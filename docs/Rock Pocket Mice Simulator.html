Directional selection (toward dark)

Directional selection shifts the overall makeup of the population by favoring variants at one extreme of the distribution. Here, lighter mice are selected against because they live among dark rocks, making it harder for them to hide from predators.

Watch for: the whole histogram sliding toward the dark end and the mean line falling.

Directional selection (toward light)

Directional selection shifts the overall makeup of the population by favoring variants at one extreme of the distribution. Here the rocks are pale, so darker mice are selected against because they stand out to predators.

Watch for: the whole histogram sliding toward the light end and the mean line rising.

Disruptive selection

Disruptive selection favors variants at both ends of the distribution. These mice have colonized a patchy habitat made up of light and dark rocks, with the result that mice of an intermediate color are selected against.

Watch for: one hump splitting into two peaks with a valley in the middle. The pink survival curve turns into a U-shape.
